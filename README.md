# AegisIQ Reporting Data Warehouse

A small, end-to-end reporting warehouse for a fictional Dota 2 coaching company: requirements → ER diagram → star schema → data mapping → Python ETL → SQL business queries → dashboard.

> **All data in this project is synthetic.** AegisIQ is a graduate course case (M.S. Business Analytics, Lewis University). I rebuilt it as a working project. The source data is generated by `etl/generate_sample_data.py` with a fixed random seed. No real company, player or payment is involved, and the findings below describe that generated data, not the real world.

## The business problem

AegisIQ sells Dota 2 coaching from **four sites** (Austin, Chicago, Seattle, Atlanta) with about 150 staff. Each site kept its own spreadsheets and exports, typed site names its own way, and counted things differently, so leadership could not compare sites or trust the totals.

The goal: **one shared reporting database** where every site reports four areas the same way.

1. **Player performance**: how players' match results (MMR) change over time
2. **Coaching sessions**: who coached whom, when, and for how long
3. **Revenue**: what each plan and session earns, by site
4. **Staffing**: how busy coaches are compared with the hours they are scheduled

## How to run it

Needs Python 3 and pandas (`pip install pandas`). From the repo folder:

```bash
python3 etl/generate_sample_data.py && python3 etl/load_warehouse.py && python3 run_queries.py
```

1. `generate_sample_data.py` writes the synthetic CSV exports to `data/raw/` (shared lists plus one folder per site).
2. `load_warehouse.py` cleans them, builds `aegisiq.db` (SQLite), and prints row counts and the data-quality check.
3. `run_queries.py` runs the 4 queries, saves each answer to `results/*.csv` and refreshes the data inside `dashboard/index.html`.

Then open `dashboard/index.html` in a browser. To explore the warehouse yourself, open `aegisiq.db` in any SQLite tool (for example DB Browser for SQLite).

## What the ETL cleans

The generator plants the kind of problems real site exports have. On the current seed, the ETL:

- **Standardises 767 site names** typed inconsistently (`austin`, `AUS`, `Seattle WA`, `Chicago ` with a trailing space …) to one official code per site. An unknown spelling stops the load instead of being guessed.
- **Removes 142 duplicate session rows** (the same `session_id` exported twice).
- **Keeps 441 blank session ratings as NULL** rather than inventing a score.
- **Computes hours booked** per coach per day from the cleaned sessions, so staffing and session numbers always agree.
- **Checks every foreign key** (0 violations).

Loaded row counts: 4 sites, 175 days, 1,140 players, 38 coaches, 20 heroes, 3 plans, 14,334 coaching sessions, 45,809 match rows, 5,108 payments, 4,557 coach shifts.

**Data-quality check (weekly sessions vs payments):** every pay-per-session session should have exactly one payment. The check compares the two counts week by week and found **41 of 2,897 pay-per-session sessions (1.4%) with no payment, spread over 19 of 25 weeks**. In a real business that list goes to finance before the revenue report is trusted. The full weekly table is in `results/dq_weekly_sessions_vs_payments.csv`.

## Findings

Period: 6 Jan – 29 Jun 2025 (25 weeks). **Synthetic data**: these numbers show what each query reports, not real business results.

**1. Do players who get more coaching improve their MMR faster?** In this data, yes: MMR gain per month rises with coaching volume.

| Sessions per month | Players | Avg MMR change / month | Win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| under 2 | 449 | −8.7 | 48.2% |
| 2–4 | 351 | −1.7 | 49.9% |
| 4–6 | 180 | +10.9 | 52.0% |
| 6+ | 160 | +25.6 | 54.4% |

Caveat: the generator deliberately raises win chance after recent sessions, so this pattern was built in. The query shows how you would test it. With real data it would show correlation, not proof that coaching causes the gain.

**2. Which site earns the most per coaching hour?** Austin earns the most per hour actually coached (**$32.89**). Seattle earns the most per hour scheduled (**$25.59**) because its coaches are almost never idle, while Atlanta earns the least on both (**$28.82** booked, **$12.84** scheduled).

| Site | Net revenue | Booked hours | Scheduled hours | $ / booked hour | $ / scheduled hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Austin | $131,578 | 4,001 | 7,212 | 32.89 | 18.24 |
| Chicago | $110,210 | 3,566 | 7,254 | 30.91 | 15.19 |
| Seattle | $146,470 | 5,026 | 5,724 | 29.14 | 25.59 |
| Atlanta | $91,848 | 3,187 | 7,152 | 28.82 | 12.84 |

**3. Are coaches overbooked or idle?** Seattle is overbooked: its 8 coaches were booked above 100% of scheduled hours in **13 of 25 weeks**, peaking at **132.9%** (week of 28 Apr). From April on, Atlanta averaged **61.6%** and Chicago **65.4%**. Over the whole period utilization was Seattle 87.8%, Austin 55.5%, Chicago 49.2%, Atlanta 44.6% (early weeks are low because players were still signing up). Action: move coaching hours from Atlanta/Chicago to Seattle, or hire for Seattle.

**4. Which plans bring the most revenue and the longest-staying players?** Monthly Pro: fewest players but the most revenue and the best retention.

| Plan | Paying players | Net revenue | Revenue / player | Avg months retained | Still active |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly Pro | 222 | $187,770 | $846 | 3.5 | 76.6% |
| Monthly Basic | 390 | $166,416 | $427 | 3.2 | 66.7% |
| Pay Per Session | 496 | $125,920 | $254 | 2.9 | 48.2% |

Total net revenue $480,106; Q2 and Q4 both add up to this total. Retention is measured up to 29 Jun 2025, so plans with more active players are undercounted slightly.

## Dashboard

`dashboard/index.html` is a single static page (Chart.js from a CDN, no paid tools) with one chart per question. `run_queries.py` writes the latest query results into the page, so it always matches `results/`.
